# Wingman — feedback and iteration log

Seven voices, one app. Four potential users walked the deployed build and the code; the
builder (an engineer-PM who has shipped Tinder-, Hinge- and Uber-class products) turned
what they found into first principles; Peter Thiel read it as chairman and commercial
adviser through the seven questions of *Zero to One*; Steve Jobs read it for UI, UX,
personality and commercial sense. Stream numbers refer to `docs/STATUS.md`. The work is not
finished until Jobs and Thiel both score it at least 9.5/10 in the same round — the
scorecards are in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-05-wingman-v4.md`, WS9.

Severity: **S1** blocks use · **S2** breaks trust or a core loop · **S3** friction · **S4** polish.

### 5 · The builder (ex-Tinder / Uber engineer-PM) — first principles

1. **Time-to-value under 60 seconds.** Install → one sentence about you → one flight → people. Anything that isn't one of those three is skippable, and BankID is *recommended*, not required, to see the board. Required to be seen by "ID-verified only" people — which is the incentive.
2. **Trust is visible in the flow, not on a settings page.** The privacy choice happens *before* the board, once, with a careful default. Block/report is on every surface that shows a person. What gets revealed at each step is said at that step.
3. **Liquidity is the product, and circles are the liquidity wedge.** An empty board kills retention; a live conference circle with 900 members guarantees density. Organiser mode is the sales tool.
4. **Double opt-in, always.** No free text before mutual yes (already true). Keep it.
5. **Scan, then read.** A board is scanned; a profile is read. Two card densities, one tap between them.
6. **Every screen ends in a next action.** No dead ends: the empty board says what to do; a settled trip says why; a closed request closes.
7. **Colour is never the only channel** (already a house rule). Terminal state, trip hue, stamp tone — all carry a label or glyph.
8. **Instrument, privately.** Local, aggregate, bucketed event counts (`seenCounts` is the start). No third-party analytics in a client-only app.
9. **One repo generation.** The v2 prototype leaves. Docs describe what is, not what was planned.
10. **Ship behind green.** `verify` + `e2e` in CI before deploy.

### 6 · Peter Thiel — chairman, commercial adviser

> "What valuable company is nobody building? Everyone builds an app for travellers. Nobody owns *the room* — the verified list of who is actually in this school, this conference, this flight. That is the secret. Own one room completely before you look at the airport."

Read through *Zero to One*'s seven questions:

| # | Question | Where Wingman stands | Finding | Stream |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Engineering** — 10× better, not 2×? | Proven admission (domain or list) + two-rule privacy is categorically different from LinkedIn's open noise and Tinder's unverified pool. The 10× is *trust per contact*, and today it is invisible: a viewer cannot tell a mocked stamp from a real one, and circles show a two-letter crest. | Make admission the hero of every circle surface: "Everyone here proved an @insead.edu address" is the product sentence, and the badge must be unforgeable in the UI. | WS3 |
| T2 | **Timing** — why now? | eID is ubiquitous in the Nordics (BankID 4.5 M users), the EU Digital Identity Wallet lands 2026, and every conference already ships an app nobody opens. | Say it in the memo and the pitch page; ship BankID-first onboarding (done in WS2). | WS2, WS9 |
| T3 | **Monopoly** — start small and dominate? | The plan is airport-first with circles as a feature. Wrong order. One school or one conference where *every* attendee is in the loop is a monopoly of a small market; the airport is the expansion, not the wedge. | Circles before trips in the order of work; an organiser can run a full event with members who have **no flight at all**. | WS3, WS4, WS6 |
| T4 | **People** — the right team? | Out of scope for the codebase; the memo lists the Mac/CI dependency and the moderation load. | — | — |
| T5 | **Distribution** — is there a channel? | Consumer install is the hardest channel there is. Selling to an organiser who pushes the invite to 1,200 badges is a channel with zero CAC. | Invite by email list, QR on the lanyard, short code on a slide; a pitch page for organisers; per-event pricing on the invite screen ("Free while in beta"). | WS3, WS6 |
| T6 | **Durability** — a moat in 10–20 years? | The verified-admission graph compounds: every circle joined makes the next admission cheaper (an INSEAD stamp is reusable). Local-only storage means nothing compounds yet. | Stamps and memberships are reusable across circles by design (already true in `privacy/`); note the Supabase seam as the point where the graph starts to exist. | WS3 |
| T7 | **Secret** — what do you know that others don't? | People will disclose more to a room they know is closed than to the open internet, and organisers will pay for the room, not the traveller. | Charge organisers; travellers are free. Never run ads. Say so on You. | WS9 |

Two more, in his words: *"Competition is for losers"* — do not position against LinkedIn or Tinder anywhere in copy; define the category ("verified rooms around the way you travel"). *"Sales matters just as much as product"* — the organiser demo (create a circle → paste 20 emails → QR → members chatting) must be flawless before anything else is polished. (T8)

### 7 · Steve Jobs — UI, UX, personality, commercial

> "Five tabs and a Requests inbox that is only requests. Where are my conversations? Make it one place. Make it obvious. Then make it beautiful."

| # | Finding | Stream |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | **One inbox.** Requests, meet rooms, circle chats and groups are four kinds of the same thing: a conversation. One list, sorted by what needs you, with a filter that is one row of chips. The Requests tab becomes **Inbox**. | WS4 |
| J2 | **Type a flight number and it fills itself in.** Forms are a failure of imagination. `SQ317` + a date should be the whole trip step for the common case. | WS5 |
| J3 | **The one sentence is the hero.** On the card it is (good). On the profile the portrait is 60 % of the screen; the app is professional-social, not a dating app — the sentence and the work line lead, the photo supports. | WS7 |
| J4 | **A circle has a face.** A two-letter crest is a placeholder. The organiser uploads the mark; the badge carries the mark; the inbox row carries the mark. | WS3 |
| J5 | **Say no.** Remove: the `Stand-in` chips from anything a viewer sees; the receipt's `dl` rows nobody reads; "Look again anyway" as a button on every settled trip. | WS4, WS7, WS9 |
| J6 | **One unforgettable moment.** Accepting a meet has a 1400 ms token reserved and nothing plays. Both cards slide in and lock, a stamp rotates in off-axis, the terminal line types itself. Once. Then never again on that screen. | WS9 |
| J7 | **It has to feel fast.** Press state on everything tappable within 130 ms (token exists, half the chips ignore it); route-level lazy loading so the first paint is the board, not 947 KB. | WS9 |
| J8 | **Personality.** The copy already has a voice ("a yes is not a commitment to keep talking"). Keep that voice everywhere and audit every string that does not sound like it. No "Submit", no "Error", no "Success". | WS9 |
| J9 | **Dark mode is not a filter.** Only the gallery has been seen dark. Every screen, both themes, before anyone calls it done. | WS9 |
| J10 | **Commercial:** the product people pay for is the organiser flow. It gets the same care as the consumer flow, and the organiser's first ten minutes decide the sale. | WS3, WS6 |

The ui-ux-pro-max checklist items adopted: 44 × 44 px touch targets with ≥ 8 px gaps · visible focus rings · 4.5:1 text contrast · skippable onboarding with Back · URL reflects state (hash routing already) · `React.lazy` for routes · no emoji as icons · `prefers-reduced-motion`. Its palette/typeface suggestion (rose + Lexend) is generic and is **not** adopted.

The `/taste` rules adopted: max one accent (ember — already), one radius system (already: pill for interactive, 16/24 for cards), full state cycles (empty/error/loading), label-above-input, no placeholder-as-label, real images not fake screenshots (the welcome page uses the unreferenced `src/assets/hero-terminal.jpg`), copy self-audit before ship, no em-dash flourishes in UI copy.
