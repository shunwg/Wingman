# Persona: Cohort sharer — Priyanka, 29

## Snapshot
- Age: 29
- Tech savviness (1–5): 5. Two-year MBA, ex-consultant. Lives in WhatsApp and a calendar. Has never read a privacy policy and never will.
- Flying habits: Fontainebleau ⇄ Paris most weekends, Singapore twice a term, and the whole cohort is on the same three flights. Books late, packs late, leaves late.
- Device: iPhone 15, one hand, walking.

## Goals
The cohort WhatsApp group has 180 people in it and every Friday somebody posts "anyone heading to CDG around 5?". Twenty messages later, three people have a car and four missed it. She wants that to take one tap instead of forty messages — and she wants to see who is in the car before she commits, because the point is arriving with someone she actually wants to talk to.

## Fears and friction
- Anything that takes more than about twenty seconds. She is doing this while walking to a lecture, and if the form has a fifth field she will go back to WhatsApp, which already works badly but works.
- Committing to something vague. "Around 5" in a group chat at least has a human on the other end; a screen that says "requested" and nothing else is worse.
- Looking like she is asking a stranger for a favour. It has to feel like splitting a cab with a classmate, not applying for something.
- Being the one who has to chase the money afterwards.

## Privacy stance (1–5)
2. She assumes the cohort is a closed group and behaves accordingly. She would be annoyed, not frightened, to learn a car was visible outside it — but she would be *properly* annoyed, because the whole reason she trusts it is that it is the cohort.

## Voice when annoyed
"Why is this asking me that?" · "Wait, who can see this?" · "I'll just put it in the group." · "Has anyone actually got the car or not?"

## Success criteria
- She can offer a car on an existing trip in under twenty seconds, without typing an address.
- She can restrict it to the cohort, and the screen tells her plainly that only the cohort can see it.
- She can tell at a glance whether a car is confirmed or still waiting on somebody — no ambiguity between "asked" and "in".
- She can see who is in the car before taking a seat.
- The money question is answered on screen without her having to raise it.
- Both directions work: to the airport for a 06:40, and out of it when the cohort lands together.
